# Daily Living & Community Participation: Building Independence and Connection

**Meta Description:** Learn how NDIS daily living support and community participation services help build independence, confidence, and meaningful community connections.

**Published:** April 2026  
**Reading Time:** 6 minutes

## Introduction

Independence and meaningful participation in community life are fundamental to wellbeing. For people with disability, NDIS daily living and community participation support can transform both.

This guide explores what these services encompass, how they work, and how they can help you live the life you want.

---

## What Is Daily Living Support?

Daily living support covers assistance with everyday tasks that help you maintain a safe, healthy, independent life at home.

### Common daily living support activities:

**Personal care:**
- Showering, bathing, grooming
- Dressing and personal hygiene
- Toileting and continence support
- Medication reminders

**Household tasks:**
- Meal preparation and cooking
- Shopping and errands
- Cleaning and laundry
- Home maintenance and safety

**Routines & planning:**
- Morning and evening routines
- Planning for the day or week
- Managing schedules and appointments
- Organising household tasks

**Financial support:**
- Bill payment assistance
- Budgeting support
- Shopping support
- Financial management help

---

## What Is Community Participation Support?

Community participation support helps you engage with your community, pursue interests, and build social connections—a crucial part of a fulfilling life.

### Common community participation activities:

**Social activities:**
- Recreation and hobbies
- Visiting friends and family
- Attending community events or classes
- Joining social groups or clubs

**Appointments & errands:**
- Transport to medical appointments
- Shopping and running errands
- School or training participation
- Employment-related activities

**Community engagement:**
- Attending community groups
- Faith or cultural groups
- Volunteering or social commitment
- Neighbourhood participation

**Confidence building:**
- Support overcoming social anxiety
- Gradually increasing independence
- Building skills for community access
- Coping strategies for challenging moments

## How Daily Living Support Works

### Your NDIS Plan

Your plan specifies:
- How many hours/weeks of daily living support you're funded for
- What types of activities are covered
- Your goals related to daily living (e.g., "maintain my home independently")
- How often support happens

### Working With Support Workers

Quality daily living support involves:

1. **Understanding your preferences**
   - How you like things done
   - Your routines and habits
   - What's important to you

2. **Gradual skill building**
   - Support workers teach as well as assist
   - The goal is often increasing independence over time
   - You're involved in planning how tasks are done

3. **Consistent, reliable service**
   - Same support worker when possible
   - Predictable timing and frequency
   - Clear communication about changes

4. **Regular review**
   - Check-ins on whether support is helping
   - Adjustments as needs or goals change
   - Progress toward independence goals

## How Community Participation Support Works

### Planning Your Participation

Your support worker helps you:
- Identify activities and interests
- Explore community options
- Plan logistics (transport, timing, cost)
- Build skills for participation

### Support Approaches

**Supported participation:**
- Worker accompanies you to activities
- Provides assistance as needed
- Gradually builds your confidence
- Steps back as you gain independence

**Preparation support:**
- Help planning what to do
- Preparation (outfit, directions, conversation starters)
- Debriefing after activities

**Building independence:**
- Teaching transport skills
- Building social skills
- Developing coping strategies
- Reducing support over time

## Benefits of Daily Living & Community Participation Support

### Building Independence

Practical support removes barriers, allowing you to develop skills and confidence. Many people gradually need less support.

### Creating Routine & Structure

Predictable daily routines:
- Reduce anxiety
- Support better sleep and wellbeing
- Build confidence and capability
- Provide stability during challenging periods

### Improving Health & Wellbeing

When daily tasks are manageable:
- You have energy for activities you enjoy
- Stress levels decrease
- Sleep and self-care improve
- Overall health benefits

### Reducing Isolation

Community participation breaks cycles of isolation:
- You build relationships and connections
- Sense of belonging increases
- Quality of life improves
- Mental wellbeing benefits

### Achieving Goals

Whether independence, employment, education, or recreation—good daily living support creates the foundation for progress.

## Real-World Examples

### Example 1: Sarah (Psychosocial Disability)

**Situation:** Sarah struggles with motivation and anxiety. Mornings are overwhelming.

**With support:** Her support worker helps her establish a consistent morning routine—gradually building confidence and independence.

**Outcome:** Within 3 months, Sarah manages mornings independently most days. Support continues when anxiety spikes.

---

### Example 2: Marcus (Physical Disability)

**Situation:** Marcus wants to attend community college but needs help with personal care and transport.

**With support:** Daily living support enables his personal care; community participation support helps with attending class and social activities.

**Outcome:** Marcus successfully completes a course and builds friendships. He's now looking at employment.

---

### Example 3: Keisha (Intellectual Disability)

**Situation:** Keisha's family wants to build her independence while ensuring she's safe.

**With support:** Support workers gradually teach life skills (cooking, shopping, safety) while she participates in community activities.

**Outcome:** Keisha now shops independently, makes some meals, and maintains social connections through regular community activities.

## What Good Daily Living & Community Participation Support Looks Like

### Person-Centred
- Your preferences and goals drive the support
- Support is tailored to your needs, not templated
- You're involved in decisions

### Gradual & Skill-Building
- Recognises that independence develops gradually
- Workers teach, not just assist
- Progress toward self-management where possible

### Respectful & Dignified
- Your privacy and autonomy are respected
- Support is provided without judgment
- You're treated as capable

### Flexible & Responsive
- Adapts to changes in your needs
- Available during difficult periods
- Scaled back as you gain independence

### Consistent & Reliable
- Same support workers when possible
- Predictable, dependable service
- Clear communication about changes

### Hopeful & Encouraging
- Workers believe in your potential
- Challenges are viewed as opportunities
- Progress is celebrated

## Setting Goals for Daily Living & Community Participation

Good goals are:

**SMART:** Specific, measurable, achievable, relevant, time-bound

**Examples of good goals:**

❌ "Get better at cooking"  
✅ "Learn to prepare 3 simple meals independently by end of plan year"

❌ "Do more community activities"  
✅ "Attend the community centre's weekly craft group fortnightly by month 3"

❌ "Improve independence"  
✅ "Manage morning routine (shower, dress, eat) independently 4 days per week"

## Questions to Ask Your Support Provider

About daily living support:
- "How is support provided respectfully?"
- "What's the goal—independence, assistance, or both?"
- "How do you adapt to my preferences and routines?"
- "How often do we review progress?"

About community participation:
- "How do you help me identify activities I enjoy?"
- "What's your approach to building independence?"
- "How do you respond if I feel anxious or overwhelmed?"
- "How do we gradually reduce support as I gain confidence?"

## Tips for Successful Community Participation

### Start Small
Start with shorter outings or lower-stakes activities. Build confidence gradually before more challenging participation.

### Choose Meaningful Activities
Engage in activities *you* care about, not just what's convenient. Genuine interest drives participation.

### Build Relationships
Consistent participation in the same activity builds relationships and belonging—often more valuable than variety.

### Develop Coping Strategies
Work with your support worker on strategies for managing anxiety or challenging moments in community settings.

### Celebrate Progress
Notice and celebrate wins—they build confidence for next steps.

### Be Patient With Yourself
Building confidence and independence takes time. Progress isn't always linear, and that's okay.

## Common Challenges & Solutions

| Challenge | Solution |
|-----------|----------|
| **Anxiety in community settings** | Start with familiar, lower-stress activities; build coping strategies; gradually attempt new settings |
| **Transport barriers** | NDIS can fund travel training; support worker can teach public transport use |
| **Lack of accessible activities** | Support worker helps identify inclusive options; advocacy for accessibility where needed |
| **Social anxiety** | Preparation and debriefing; building social skills; starting with structured activities |
| **Physical fatigue** | Plan realistic frequency; balance activities with rest; adjust support as needed |
| **Motivation fluctuations** | Acknowledge this is normal; maintain gentle consistency; adjust activities as needed |

## Planning Your Support

When discussing daily living and community participation in your NDIS plan:

### Share Your Vision
- What independence matters most to you?
- What community activities do you enjoy or want to try?
- What barriers prevent full participation?

### Be Specific
- Which daily tasks need support?
- How often do you need community participation support?
- Where should activities happen?

### Think Long-Term
- What skills do you want to develop?
- How might independence increase over your plan period?
- What's your ultimate vision?

Daily living and community participation support are about far more than assistance—they're about:
- **Building independence** where possible

- **Supporting you to live the life you want**

Quality support recognises your potential and focuses on gradual progression toward greater independence and participation.

If you're exploring these supports, take time to find a provider who genuinely understands your goals and is committed to helping you achieve them.
